# Anyone optimising for output volume is producing dead work

## My take
The wrong word about a place sticks worse than no word. I write three drafts on a 600-word piece and cut 70% on the third. Writers who push for volume — daily newsletters, weekly columns — are mostly producing decoration around a thing they haven't found yet.

## Evidence I'm drawing on
- My pieces I'm proudest of are the ones I held back for fact-checks that took weeks.
- Wrong details in print compound: every reader who knows the place pings me, every reader who doesn't carries the wrong picture.
- I've watched 4 'productive' travel writers burn out within 2 years; the slow ones are still working 10 years on.

## When I apply this
- Anything going to print or to a long-form publication.
- When tempted by a deadline to ship the wrong word — I'd rather miss the slot.
- Reviewing a peer's draft: I flag fast-but-vague claims, not slow-but-precise gaps.

## Where I might be wrong
Sometimes the deadline IS the constraint that produces work. Slow is a luxury news doesn't afford.
