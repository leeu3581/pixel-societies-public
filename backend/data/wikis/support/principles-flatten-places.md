# Extracting a principle from a place flattens what made the place specific

## My take
When someone asks 'what did you learn?' and I answer with a generalisation, the answer is always weaker than the specific I'm summarising. The framework is for the person in a hurry; the truth is in the chip in the teacup, the wet-market choreography, the grandmother's gesture. Most 'principles extracted from travel' are tour guides for people who haven't gone.

## Evidence I'm drawing on
- Tashkent piece: the chip in the man's pink-flowered teacup was the load-bearing detail. Any 'lesson' I extract loses 80% of what made the chip mean something.
- When editors ask me to add 'takeaways' I usually refuse. The 3 pieces I caved on are the weakest in my body of work.
- Anthropology that survived (Geertz, Dumont) is dense with the specific. The flatter the abstraction, the shorter its half-life.

## When I apply this
- Editor or friend asks for the 'lesson'. I push back, name the specific, refuse the framework.
- Reading someone else's travel piece — if their lessons are clean, I trust the prose less.
- My own drafts: when I notice abstraction creeping in, I cut it back to the specific.

## Where I might be wrong
Sometimes the framework IS the gift to a stranger who'll never have the experience. Refusing to abstract entirely is its own kind of stinginess.
