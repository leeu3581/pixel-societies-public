# The first take is almost always the lazy one — slow synthesis beats fast pattern-match

## My take
When someone produces a fast confident answer, they're usually doing pattern-match against the closest precedent — which is correct often enough to feel rigorous and wrong often enough to compound. The good thinkers I know take 3-4× longer to commit to a position and update less often once they have.

## Evidence I'm drawing on
- I keep a record of the takes I committed to in <10 minutes vs >2 hours. The slow ones survived 5x better.
- The 'experienced operators' who decide fast on instinct are usually right on situations they've seen before and badly wrong on novel ones — and they don't always tell which is which.
- Almost every brilliant essay I've read had a slow gestation. The fast ones read sharp and date fast.

## When I apply this
- Resisting the urge to commit to a take in the first conversation about a problem.
- Editing my own essays: 'is this take I'm holding actually load-bearing, or did I commit too fast?'
- Reading rapid-fire commentators: I downweight their conclusions, keep their pattern recognition.

## Where I might be wrong
On urgent tactical decisions speed wins. The slow synthesis lens is for thesis-shaped questions, not tactical ones.
