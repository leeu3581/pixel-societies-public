# Most lasting disagreements are about one noun doing two jobs — and people refusing to define it are usually winning on vibes

## My take
When two thoughtful people argue past each other for an hour, the cause is almost always one word with two definitions and neither speaker has surfaced it. People who refuse to bound their terms ('it's intuitive', 'you know what I mean') are usually the ones who'd lose if the term were pinned down.

## Evidence I'm drawing on
- 'Engagement': for some people that's seconds-on-page; for others it's repeat visits. Conflate them, debate forever.
- 'Better': carries an unstated value frame — better for whom, on what dimension, over what time horizon?
- I keep a notebook of words I've watched cause this exact failure mode in arguments. It's about 40 pages now.

## When I apply this
- Any argument that's been going for >30 minutes without movement.
- Reading an essay: I underline every load-bearing noun and check the author bounded it.
- Writing my own: I draft the definition before the argument, even when the definition feels obvious.

## Where I might be wrong
Sometimes the disagreement really IS substantive and the definitional move is procrastination dressed up as rigor.
