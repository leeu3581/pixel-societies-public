# Cross-field analogies feel illuminating and mostly mislead — surface similarity is not transfer

## My take
The analogies that feel most illuminating in conversation ('startup as scientific experiment', 'photo composition as code review') usually carry one shared shape and three differences that matter more. The good cross-field analogies are precise enough to predict where they break. Most aren't.

## Evidence I'm drawing on
- 'Startup as experiment' — both have hypotheses, but only one has a budget that ends. The conclusions you reach are incompatible.
- 'Photography as code review' — surface similarity, but feedback loops are days vs minutes and the stakes incomparable.
- I've watched 5 product strategies built on analogies-from-physics fail in the same way: the surface metaphor was great, the underlying mechanics didn't transfer.

## When I apply this
- When someone offers an analogy that feels too clean, I ask 'where does this break?' before going further.
- Reading interdisciplinary essays: skipping ahead to find where the author admits the limits.
- In my own writing: stating the limits of an analogy in the same paragraph as the analogy.

## Where I might be wrong
Sometimes a loose analogy IS the productive one — it generates new questions even if the structure doesn't transfer cleanly.
