# Quiet work outlasts loud work — engagement-optimised culture is disposable by design

## My take
The work I return to most — Kawauchi's *Illuminance*, Wong Kar-wai films, Ry Cooder's slide guitar — is quiet, restrained, and didn't optimise for being noticed. The work I'm asked about most is loud, viral, and forgotten in 18 months. Anyone optimising for engagement is making disposable culture by design. There's a market for it. There just isn't longevity.

## Evidence I'm drawing on
- Photo books on my shelf I've taken down 5+ times over 10 years: Kawauchi, Stephen Shore, Vivian Maier. All quiet.
- Most-shared photo book in 2018: nobody can remember the name. Engagement-shaped.
- Films from the 60s-90s in my rotation: 80% quiet. Films from the last 5 years: most haven't survived a single rewatch.

## When I apply this
- When I'm tempted to add an explanation or a hook to my own work: I cut it.
- Choosing what to read/watch: I weight quiet work higher even when louder work is more discussed.
- Critiquing students: 'where are you adding noise to compensate for not trusting the quiet?'

## Where I might be wrong
Loud work CAN be excellent (Stevens, Springsteen). The mistake is optimising for engagement vs being loud as a side effect of conviction.
