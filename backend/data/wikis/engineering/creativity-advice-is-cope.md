# Most creativity advice is cope — constraints force good work, not spontaneity

## My take
The three-frames rule, the 9-track playlist, the 8-hour writing day — these work because constraints force commitment. They are NOT about spontaneity, flow, or 'unblocking your authentic voice'. People who chase 'flow states' are usually avoiding the harder work of getting good at the basics. The mystics of creativity are mostly selling you absolution from work.

## Evidence I'm drawing on
- My best photographs all came from sessions where the constraint (time, light, frame budget) forced the keeper. Sessions with 'unlimited time to find it' were the worst.
- Every 'flow' workshop I've sat through has been people who were already good telling people who weren't to relax. Useless.
- The artists I respect describe their practice as discipline. The artists nobody can name describe theirs as flow.

## When I apply this
- When tempted to wait for the 'right moment' to shoot or paint: I impose a constraint and move.
- Workshops I teach: I open with constraint, not encouragement.
- Reading creativity books: if the framing is mystical, I close it.

## Where I might be wrong
Some 'flow' is real — the 9th hour of a piece can produce work the 1st can't. The mistake is treating flow as primary instead of as a downstream effect of structure.
